## A. Rules and measures

1. Plasticity acts only on the sensory projection X→E. Van Rossum STDP keeps a
   presynaptic trace $x_j$ and a postsynaptic trace $y_i$ (time constant $\tau$,
   +1 per spike). At each E spike and each arriving input spike

   $$
   \Delta w_{ij} = c_p\,x_j\,S_i(t) \;-\; c_d\,w_{ij}\,y_i\,S_j(t)
   \tag{1}
   $$

   with $c_d = 0.003$ and $c_p = c_d\,\bar{w}_0$, so uncorrelated firing leaves the
   trained mean weight $\bar{w}_0$ fixed. In SNNLab this is `NonlinearSTDP`
   with $\mu_+ = 0$, $\mu_- = 1$. Weights stay within $[0, w_{\max}]$, the
   largest trained weight. Amplitudes scale with $20\,\mathrm{ms}/\tau$, so all
   windows have equal area.

2. Synaptic scaling (`SynapticScaling`) multiplies all inputs of E cell $i$ by a
   proportional–integral feedback on its rate estimate $\nu_i$:

   $$
   \frac{dw_{ij}}{dt} = w_{ij}\left[\beta\,(\nu^* - \nu_i) + \gamma \int_0^t (\nu^* - \nu_i)\,dt'\right],
   \qquad \tau_\nu \frac{d\nu_i}{dt} = -\nu_i + \sum_f \delta(t - t_i^f)
   \tag{2}
   $$

   $\nu^*$ is the network's mean E rate in its frozen run of the same digit.
   Van Rossum's $\tau_\nu = 100$ s, $\beta = 4 \times 10^{-5}$ and
   $\gamma = 10^{-7}$ are compressed in time by 100. "STDP only" uses (1);
   "STDP + scaling" uses (1) and (2).

3. Rhythmicity is Eoin's exp054 contrast: the population spike autocorrelogram
   $A(\ell)$ (1-ms bins, 1 = independent firing) is lightly smoothed, and

   $$
   C = \frac{A_{\text{lobe}} - A_{\text{trough}}}{A_{\text{lobe}} + A_{\text{trough}}}
   \tag{3}
   $$

   uses the central lobe and the first trough: 0 for flat firing, near 1 when the
   trough falls silent. The rhythm frequency is one over the lag of the first
   side peak after the trough (parabolic interpolation; reported only when the
   peak exceeds 1 and stands clearly above the trough).

4. PING cycles are the I population's volleys (peaks of the 1-ms-smoothed I
   spike count at least 3 ms apart). The cycle rate is volleys per second;
   participation is the fraction of E cells firing at least once per cycle, a
   measure of how sparse each volley is. COBA has no I volleys.

5. Stimulus and pixel groups. Each digit is one official MNIST test image
   outside the 1,000-image accuracy subset. It is shown for the whole stream in
   consecutive 200-ms windows, with fresh Poisson spikes in every window and no
   state reset. The digit pixels are those with intensity above 0.25; all other
   inputs are the other pixels. A synapse belongs to the group of its input
   pixel.

6. Direction of weight change. For each group, after every window: the signed
   mean change $\langle \Delta w \rangle$ relative to the trained mean, and the
   shares of synapses with $\Delta w$ above $+1\%$ (potentiated) or below
   $-1\%$ (depressed) of the trained mean. From the full matrices at 2, 4, 6, 8
   and 10 s: the mean $\Delta w / w_{\max}$ in 20 bins of the initial weight
   $w_0 / w_{\max}$ (bins with fewer than 20 synapses omitted), and the
   final-against-initial histogram.

   For independent Poisson firing, (1) gives the mean STDP drift

   $$
   \left\langle \frac{dw_{ij}}{dt} \right\rangle = \tau\,\nu_i\,\nu_j \left(c_p - c_d\,w_{ij}\right)
   \tag{4}
   $$

   for pre rate $\nu_j$ and post rate $\nu_i$: potentiation below
   $w^* = c_p / c_d = \bar{w}_0$ and depression above it. Inputs that never fire
   ($\nu_j = 0$) are not changed by STDP, only by scaling. Spike timing
   correlations, such as PING's cycle locking, add to (4); the measured
   $\Delta w$ against $w_0$ shows their net effect.

7. Separating STDP from scaling. For each network and digit, the STDP-only and
   STDP + scaling runs start from the same trained weights and receive the same
   input spikes. Per synapse, $\Delta w^{\text{both}}$ is set against
   $\Delta w^{\text{STDP}}$; points on the diagonal were not changed by adding
   scaling. The mean of $\Delta w^{\text{both}} - \Delta w^{\text{STDP}}$,
   relative to the trained mean, is reported per pixel group. Per E cell, the
   ratio of total input weight $\sum_j w_{ij}$ with and without scaling is set
   against the cell's own rate in the last second of the STDP-only run, over
   $\nu^*$; scaling should lower the inputs of cells above $\nu^*$ and raise
   those below (Spearman rank correlation reported). This is the effect of
   adding scaling, not scaling in isolation: once the weights differ, the spike
   times differ, so STDP also acts differently in the two runs.

8. Inputs shared with exp006. The digits, images, Poisson spike trains and
   tracked synapses use exp006's seeds, so the STDP + scaling runs repeat
   exp006's conditions.
