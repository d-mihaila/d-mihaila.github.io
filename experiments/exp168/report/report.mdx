## Abstract

We compared spiking E/I networks with EE/II recurrence enabled or absent. Both used the same timestep, duration, seed, populations, and neuron parameters. In each case, changing three external-drive settings produced a low-correlation AI-like state and a strongly rhythmic PING state. Thus both regimes can appear with or without EE/II recurrence.

In a third comparison, the dense-input EE/II-on network transitions from AI-like
activity to a strongly rhythmic PING-like state by reducing only external input
gain to I, while wiring, sampled weights and source rate remain fixed.

### Gain-only transition with fixed wiring

The dense-input network from Figure 1B also supports a transition within one
continuous trajectory. Reducing direct external excitation of I produces strong
population oscillations without changing connectivity or recurrent coupling.
EE and II remain present and active throughout. This follows the input-gain
control used in [exp148](/exp148); it demonstrates a transition through reduced
I drive, rather than increased overall input drive.
