## A. Van Rossum learning rule

1. The rule acts only on the sensory projection X→E. Each input channel $j$ keeps a
   presynaptic trace $x_j$ and each E cell $i$ keeps a postsynaptic trace $y_i$, both
   with time constant $\tau = 20$ ms and incremented by 1 per spike. At each E spike
   and each arriving input spike the weight $w_{ij}$ changes by

   $$
   \Delta w_{ij} = c_p\,x_j\,S_i(t) \;-\; c_d\,w_{ij}\,y_i\,S_j(t)
   \tag{1}
   $$

   where $S$ marks a spike in the current step. Potentiation is a constant step
   $c_p$; depression is proportional to the weight, with $c_d = 0.003$
   (van Rossum, Bi and Turrigiano, 2000). In SNNLab this is `NonlinearSTDP` with
   $\mu_+ = 0$, $\mu_- = 1$, $a_- = c_d\,w_{\max}$ and $a_+ = c_p$.

2. For uncorrelated pre- and postsynaptic firing the mean drift vanishes at
   $w^* = c_p / c_d$. We set $c_p = c_d\,\bar{w}_0$, the trained mean weight, so the
   rule does not move the mean by itself; only spike-timing correlations do.
   Weights are clipped to $[0, w_{\max}]$, with $w_{\max}$ the largest trained weight.

3. Synaptic scaling (`SynapticScaling`) multiplies all inputs of E cell $i$ by a
   proportional–integral feedback on its rate estimate $\nu_i$:

   $$
   \frac{dw_{ij}}{dt} = w_{ij}\left[\beta\,(\nu^* - \nu_i) + \gamma \int_0^t (\nu^* - \nu_i)\,dt'\right],
   \qquad \tau_\nu \frac{d\nu_i}{dt} = -\nu_i + \sum_f \delta(t - t_i^f)
   \tag{2}
   $$

   The target $\nu^*$ is the network's mean E rate in the frozen stream. Van Rossum
   used $\tau_\nu = 100$ s, $\beta = 4 \times 10^{-5}$ s⁻¹ Hz⁻¹ and
   $\gamma = 10^{-7}$ s⁻² Hz⁻¹. Our stream lasts seconds, so we compress time by
   100: $\tau_\nu = 1$ s, $\beta = 4 \times 10^{-3}$ and $\gamma = 10^{-3}$.
   STDP amplitudes are per spike pair and are not compressed.
